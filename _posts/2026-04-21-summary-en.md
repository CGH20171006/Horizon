## [Why AES-128 Still Resists Quantum Attack](https://words.filippo.io/128-bits/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Filippo Valsorda published an explainer arguing that quantum computers are not a practical threat to 128-bit symmetric keys, pushing back on the common claim that Grover’s algorithm simply turns AES-128 into “64-bit security.” The article reframes the issue around real attack cost, fault-tolerant quantum overhead, and the limits of using Grover’s algorithm for brute-force key search. This matters because many security discussions oversimplify quantum risk and may push teams toward unnecessary migrations or misleading security assumptions. For practitioners planning post-quantum defenses, the article helps separate the urgent threat to public-key cryptography from the much weaker and more expensive impact on symmetric encryption. Grover’s algorithm gives only a quadratic speedup for unstructured search, so it does not “break” AES-128 in the same way Shor’s algorithm threatens RSA and elliptic-curve systems. Practical quantum key search would also require enormous fault-tolerant resources, and published resource analyses emphasize that runtime, qubit count, circuit depth, and parallelization overhead strongly affect whether such an attack is plausible.

hackernews · hasheddan · Apr 20, 16:37

**Background**: Grover’s algorithm is a quantum algorithm for unstructured search that reduces the number of oracle queries from roughly N to roughly square root of N, which is why people often say it halves the effective bit strength of symmetric keys. However, that rule of thumb describes asymptotic query complexity, not the full engineering cost of a real cryptanalytic system. In contrast, Shor’s algorithm threatens widely used public-key systems by exploiting mathematical structure, which is why post-quantum migration is primarily focused on replacing RSA and elliptic-curve cryptography. Recent AES resource estimates also show that implementing Grover-based key search on fault-tolerant quantum hardware involves large overheads and difficult trade-offs.
