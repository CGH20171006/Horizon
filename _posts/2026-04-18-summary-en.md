## [Ancient DNA study finds widespread recent human selection](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10358-1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A Nature paper analyzed 15,836 ancient West Eurasian genomes, including 10,016 newly reported samples, and estimated selection coefficients at 9.7 million variants across the last 10,000 years. The authors report that directional selection was pervasive rather than rare, with allele-frequency shifts linked to lower predicted body fat and schizophrenia risk and higher cognition-related measures in present-day humans. This study challenges a long-standing view in human evolutionary genetics that strong recent directional selection is uncommon, suggesting that natural selection has shaped many more loci and complex traits than previously recognized. It also shows how large ancient DNA time series can move beyond reconstructing population history to directly test how evolution influenced modern human biology. According to the Nature report, the method tests for consistent allele-frequency trends over time while accounting for population structure, addressing a central difficulty in separating selection from migration or demographic change. The study focuses on West Eurasia, so its conclusions are strongest for that region, and trait-related inferences are based on genetic associations rather than direct measurement of ancient individuals’ phenotypes.

telegram · zaihuapd · Apr 17, 18:00

**Background**: Ancient DNA studies compare genomes from people who lived at different times, allowing researchers to observe how allele frequencies changed across millennia instead of inferring the past only from living populations. Directional selection refers to a sustained increase or decrease in the frequency of variants that affect reproductive success, but in human history such trends can be confounded by migrations and population mixing. Earlier ancient-genome selection scans in West Eurasia found notable cases, but limited sample sizes made it difficult to determine how widespread this process was. This paper uses a much larger dataset and a dedicated statistical framework to revisit that question at genome scale.

## [iTerm2 makes viewing text potentially dangerous](https://blog.calif.io/p/mad-bugs-even-cat-readmetxt-is-not) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A security writeup showed that iTerm2 can be exploited by malicious escape sequences embedded in file contents, so even a simple command like `cat readme.txt` may trigger unsafe behavior when rendered in the terminal. The issue affects iTerm2’s handling of terminal control sequences, and the post notes that a fix had not yet reached stable releases at the time of disclosure. This matters because terminal emulators are trusted developer tools, and many users assume displaying a text file is harmless. If terminal output itself can become an attack surface, developers, operators, and anyone inspecting untrusted logs or repository files may be exposed to phishing, UI deception, or worse through normal workflows. iTerm2 supports many proprietary escape codes, including OSC-based features such as file, image, and other rich interactions, and its own documentation warns these sequences may have unknown effects and should be used with caution. The broader class of bugs is not limited to memory-safety issues: terminal parsers and feature-rich integrations can have logic flaws where rendering attacker-controlled text causes unintended actions.

hackernews · arkadiyt · Apr 17, 18:43

**Background**: Terminal emulators interpret ANSI and related escape sequences that are embedded directly in text streams, allowing programs to change colors, move the cursor, set titles, or invoke emulator-specific features. iTerm2 extends this model with proprietary escape codes such as OSC 1337 for richer capabilities, which increases convenience but also expands the attack surface if untrusted content is displayed. Security researchers have repeatedly shown that terminals can be abused through escape-sequence handling, including earlier iTerm2 issues and other terminal-emulator URL or command-handling bugs.

## [NIST narrows NVD CVE enrichment](https://risky.biz/risky-bulletin-nist-gives-up-enriching-most-cves/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NIST is reportedly changing how the National Vulnerability Database handles CVE records, scaling back enrichment for most entries instead of adding detailed metadata and scoring across the board. According to NIST’s latest update, it will focus enrichment on selected CVEs that meet specific criteria, while more scoring and record detail will come from vendors and other CVE issuers. This changes a core input used by vulnerability management tools, defenders, and downstream databases that have long relied on NVD for normalized severity and metadata. If vendors and other issuers provide more of that information directly, the ecosystem could get faster coverage in some cases but also more inconsistency, conflicts of interest, and trust issues around severity scoring. NIST says the shift is tied to record CVE growth, with one report citing a 263% surge in vulnerability submissions, which makes full enrichment of every record harder to sustain. In NVD terminology, “enrichment” means adding information such as CVSS severity metrics, affected product data, and other analysis beyond the base CVE record.

hackernews · mooreds · Apr 17, 15:09

**Background**: CVE is the standard identifier system for publicly disclosed software vulnerabilities, and CVE Numbering Authorities, or CNAs, are authorized organizations that can assign CVE IDs and publish CVE records. The NVD, run by NIST, has historically taken those CVE records and enriched them with additional analysis, including CVSS severity data and other metadata used by security tools. CVSS is a widely used scoring framework that assigns a numerical severity score from 0 to 10, but it has long been debated because the same vulnerability can be interpreted differently depending on context and scoring choices.

## [Chinese team creates artificial ball-lightning analogue](https://www.news.cn/tech/20260416/b487a8967b70495f8fc00364844225d6/c.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers at the Shanghai Institute of Optics and Fine Mechanics of the Chinese Academy of Sciences reported the first artificial generation and capture of a spherical luminous object closely resembling natural ball lightning. Published in Nature Photonics on April 16, the experiment provides evidence that ball lightning is an electromagnetic soliton. Ball lightning has long been a controversial and poorly verified atmospheric phenomenon, so an experimentally reproducible analogue is scientifically significant. The result could matter beyond atmospheric physics because it sheds light on how extreme electromagnetic energy can be self-confined in plasma, a topic relevant to high-energy-density physics and potentially to fusion research. According to the report, the team used an intense laser to drive terahertz waves to relativistic-level local field strength, ionizing argon gas into plasma and trapping energy through a balance between optical radiation pressure and thermal pressure. The resulting energy sphere had a diameter of about 100 micrometers and a lifetime of about 100 nanoseconds; the researchers argue that scaling laws map it to natural ball lightning with diameters of tens of centimeters lasting several seconds.

telegram · zaihuapd · Apr 17, 09:00

**Background**: A soliton is a localized wave packet that can maintain its shape because dispersive spreading is balanced by nonlinear effects. In plasma physics, confinement refers to keeping plasma energy from rapidly dispersing, often by balancing different pressure forces or electromagnetic effects. Ball lightning is a rare luminous spherical phenomenon reported during thunderstorms, but its mechanism has remained uncertain because it is difficult to observe directly and even harder to reproduce in the laboratory.
