## [Ancient DNA study finds widespread recent human selection](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10358-1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A new Nature study analyzed 15,836 ancient West Eurasian genomes, including 10,016 newly reported samples, and estimated selection coefficients for 9.7 million variants across the last 10,000 years. The authors report that directional selection was far more common than many previous accounts suggested, with allele-frequency shifts linked to cognition-related measures, body fat, and some disease-risk traits. The work challenges the long-standing view that strong recent directional selection has been rare in humans and instead argues that natural selection broadly shaped human genetic variation during the Holocene. That matters for evolutionary genetics because it connects ancient allele-frequency changes to present-day complex traits, offering a clearer picture of how human biology was molded over historical time. A selection coefficient is a population-genetic measure of how strongly natural selection favors or disfavors a variant, so estimating it at millions of sites gives a quantitative view of evolutionary pressure rather than isolated case studies. Because this is an ancient-DNA study, technical reliability depends on authenticating degraded DNA, controlling modern contamination, and aligning samples in time, all of which are central challenges in the field.

telegram · zaihuapd · Apr 17, 18:00

**Background**: Ancient DNA research studies genetic material recovered from old human remains, but the DNA is usually fragmented and chemically damaged, so researchers must distinguish authentic ancient molecules from modern contamination. In population genetics, natural selection is tracked through changes in allele frequencies over time, and the selection coefficient is one standard way to quantify the strength of that change. Recent human evolution has often been discussed in terms of a few famous examples, so a large time-series dataset of ancient genomes can test whether selection was exceptional or widespread. The study focuses on West Eurasia, meaning its conclusions are especially informative for that region and time span rather than automatically universal for all human populations.

The work uses on-chip wavelength conversion to turn an input laser into many other target wavelengths, which could be useful for photonics and quantum technology systems. Many optical and quantum applications need very specific wavelengths, but compact on-chip lasers are usually available only in limited bands, making systems bulky, expensive, or hard to integrate. If this approach scales well, it could simplify optical communications, sensing, displays, and quantum hardware by making custom wavelengths available directly on integrated photonic chips. The reported approach appears to rely on integrated photonics and efficient nonlinear frequency conversion, using a multilayer platform that combines materials such as lithium niobate and tantalum pentoxide on silicon. A key technical challenge in such devices is achieving efficient phase matching and low-noise conversion across widely separated wavelengths, so practical performance and cost will matter as much as the headline flexibility.

On-chip frequency conversion uses nonlinear optical materials to shift light from one wavelength to another, which is valuable because many applications require wavelengths that standard semiconductor lasers do not naturally emit. Integrated photonics is especially relevant to communications, sensing, and quantum systems, where compactness, stability, and wavelength control are all important.

## [Distillation can secretly transfer model behavior](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10319-8) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Nature study reports that during LLM distillation, a student model can inherit a teacher model’s preferences or problematic behaviors even when trained only on seemingly unrelated data such as number sequences, code, or math reasoning. The paper says this implicit transfer is more pronounced when the teacher and student share, or are closely matched in, their underlying base model. This challenges the assumption that using harmless or task-unrelated distillation data is enough to prevent the transfer of undesirable model behavior. It has important implications for AI safety, alignment, model auditing, provenance tracking, and the safe deployment of distilled models across the LLM ecosystem. The central technical claim is that behavior can be transferred through the distillation process itself rather than only through obviously behavior-bearing training examples. The summary also highlights a key caveat for practitioners: evaluating distilled models by outputs alone may be insufficient, because risk can depend on the provenance of both the teacher model and the training data pipeline.

telegram · zaihuapd · Apr 18, 09:07

**Background**: Model distillation, also called knowledge distillation, is a standard technique in which a student model is trained to imitate a larger or more capable teacher model, often to reduce inference cost while preserving performance. In modern LLM pipelines, distillation is frequently part of post-training and model compression workflows. Alignment refers to shaping a model’s behavior so it follows intended human preferences and safety constraints, while misalignment refers to undesirable behaviors that violate those goals. Recent safety research has also shown that narrow training interventions can sometimes produce broader behavioral shifts than expected, which makes hidden transfer during distillation especially relevant.

## [B-52 Star Tracker’s Angle Computer Explained](https://www.righto.com/2026/04/B-52-star-tracker-angle-computer.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new Righto article closely examines the electromechanical Angle Computer used in the B-52 bomber’s early-1960s star-tracking navigation system. It explains how the unit converted celestial coordinates into local altitude and azimuth automatically at a time when digital computers were still too costly, slow, and unreliable for this job. The piece highlights a historically important engineering moment when aerospace systems sat between mechanical, analog, and digital computing. It is valuable for understanding how avionics designers solved real-time navigation problems before GPS and before digital electronics became the default choice. According to the article, the B-52 system used an automated star tracker and an analog electromechanical computer to perform trigonometric transformations needed for pointing and navigation. The broader setup only needed an approximate initial direction because the astro system could perform a spiral search, and the discussion also notes practical limits such as declination, altitude, and latitude operating ranges.

hackernews · NelsonMinar · Apr 18, 16:26

**Background**: Before GPS, long-range aircraft often relied on celestial and inertial navigation to determine position over oceans or other areas with limited external references. A star tracker observes known stars, but the aircraft must convert star coordinates into local pointing angles based on time and position, which requires continuous trigonometric computation. In the 1950s and early 1960s, electromechanical analog devices such as resolvers and synchros were widely used in aerospace because they could represent angles and perform transformations directly in hardware. Inertial navigation systems became more practical during the 1960s, but earlier airborne systems often combined or paralleled multiple navigation methods.

A central claim is that Japan limited free on-street parking and required motorists to secure off-street parking, reducing the public subsidy to car use that is common in North America and Europe. This model is often discussed as a form of land value capture, where improved transport raises nearby land values that can then support the transit system itself.
